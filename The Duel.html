An AY interpretation of the complete MIDI transcription. Cello and computer lead parts take priority; orchestration is reduced to three square-wave voices. The film's acoustic timbres and exact synchronization are not reproduced.

Inspect chip registers

R0–R5: tone periods · R6: noise · R7: mixer · R8–R10: amplitudes · R11–R12: envelope period · R13: shape. These are the values sent to the chip. FF in R13 means “no write at this instant,” keeping the envelope running.

The arrangement and its limits

The Duel: based on Richard L. Monts’ complete MIDI transcription, including 374 cello notes, 405 computer notes, 254 tempo events and 656 pitch-bend events. The source tempo map, note onsets, volume changes, modulation and computer bends drive a register sequence at 1,000 Hz. Timing is rounded to the nearest millisecond and pitches to the AY’s integer dividers. Bow-like attacks and vibrato approximate the cello; a sharper envelope distinguishes the computer.

Channel A carries the cello and B the computer. C gives priority to simultaneous cello double-stops, then source bass, ostinato and strings; brief noise or tone bursts add percussion. Much of the orchestral layering is necessarily omitted. Brief legato overlaps in each lead are resolved to the newer note. The initial silent MIDI count-in is removed and a short decay tail is added, giving 3:46 of playback. This transcription differs in timing from the 4:04 film clip; it has not been aligned to or aurally verified against that recording.

Together in Electric Dreams: the earlier 271-note melody adaptation remains available at 130 BPM and 200 Hz register timing. It starts at the vocal section, with reconstructed bass, harmony and percussion; it is not the complete recording.

The sound core emulates three square-wave generators, 12-bit tone dividers, a 17-bit noise LFSR, tone/noise gating, 16 amplitude levels and all 16 shared-envelope shapes. It integrates digital transitions, uses 4× oversampling with a fourth-order low-pass filter, and removes DC. It approximates the analog output and is not calibrated against a physical AY or a particular computer’s output circuit. Stereo and channel faders are external mixing controls.

WAV exports the entire piece at 48 kHz / 16 bits with the current mix. YM exports the chip register sequence without those external controls; its player must honor the header’s 1,000 Hz (Duel) or 200 Hz (Together) rate. Import supports uncompressed YM2, YM3, YM3b, YM5 and YM6 without digidrums or extra effects. This player does not emulate a Z80 or execute .ay machine-code files.

Everything runs offline. The first Play prepares the audio locally; changing the piece, clock, octave or percussion rebuilds it. Use Space to play or pause when focus is outside a control. Headphones make the exchange between the two leads easier to follow.
